# Judge finished streams by reactions, not kills

CutCut should choose clips from what a finished stream actually records: the streamer's voice, the audience's chat and the transcript, with an LLM judging a shortlist. It should not chase kill-feed or HUD data. The current pipeline has the right shape, and its best decision, measuring chat lag per video, has the strongest evidence behind it. The 35/50/15 weights are guesses, though, and two details hurt: **chat keeps its 50% share even when it is too thin to register, and raw loudness rewards gunfire**. Fix those, add chat-repetition and speech-gated voice signals (both free and local), transcribe the whole stream (about **$0.16 for four hours** at Groq's listed Turbo rate), and add vision only after a labelled test set shows what it buys. Medal and the other local apps depend on game events that do not exist for a YouTube link, so the real competitors are Eklipse, StreamLadder, OpusClip and above all YouTube's own AI highlights. CutCut's defensible edge is inspectable picks driven by YouTube chat replay, at bring-your-own-key cost on long streams. Almost all quantitative evidence comes from League of Legends and Dota esports broadcasts, none of it was validated on YouTube variety streams, and CutCut has never run on a real video, so every verdict below is provisional until a small labelled set exists.

Throughout, **Documented** means a vendor's help page, a policy text or a paper's measured result; **Vendor claim** means marketing or a competitor's statement; **Inference** means my reasoning. Evidence grades mean: Strong (quantified gaming results from more than one peer-reviewed or examined source), Moderate (one quantified gaming source or preprint), Weak (anecdote, vendor statement or non-gaming result), None (engineering judgement). Code findings come from reading `aai/src/highlights/` directly.

## The architecture survives, but the weights and several details do not

The most useful way to read the pipeline is as two stages. The measured score only chooses which candidates (up to 4x the clip count, capped at 30) reach the LLM. Once an LLM key is present, `applyVerdicts` ranks by the model's own score and the fused number is discarded. **So the 35/50/15 weights are a recall setting, not a quality setting**, except on no-key runs where they decide everything (Inference from `judge.ts`). That changes what to tune: the question is whether the good moments make the shortlist.

Multimodal fusion is the right bet. On LoL Worlds, audio, visual and chat together reached F1 0.642 against 0.432 to 0.534 for single modalities, and the worst single video rose to 0.410 from 0.178 (audio) and 0.074 (chat), which matters more for a product serving many streamers than the headline gain ([Ringer 2022](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/32406/1/PhD_Thesis__Revised_.pdf)). Ringer cautions that the chat model was tuned on Worlds data and the audio-visual models were not, so this does not rank the modalities. Lag correction, smoothing and thresholding each moved scores about as much as adding a modality, so they come first.

### Where the code diverges from the evidence

**Thin chat is dead weight, and the code cannot see it.** `fuseSignals` re-normalises only when chat is entirely absent. I ran a copy of `signals.ts` on synthetic chat with plain, single-weight messages: at background rates up to 2,000 messages an hour, a burst of **5 distinct chatters in 5 seconds scored 0 to 0.07, 10 scored 0.17 to 0.30, and 20 scored 0.46 to 0.75**, because the `sqrt(base+1)` floor and the z-score mapping demand an absolute surge. A 20-viewer stream rarely produces that, yet chat still takes 50% of the score (59% with no heatmap), capping audio-only moments at 0.41. LIGHTOR states it needs more than 500 messages an hour ([LIGHTOR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12201)). Until a labelled set exists, use audio 0.45, chat 0.45 x min(1, messages per hour / 500), heatmap 0.10, then re-normalise. These numbers are my placeholder, justified only by the absence of evidence favouring either side.

**The agreement bonus behaves oddly at both ends.** Without a heatmap, audio 0.39 with chat 1.0 scores 0.75, while audio 0.40 with chat 1.0 scores 0.94. A maxed chat-only spike (0.59) also outranks moderate agreement at 0.4/0.4 (0.50), contradicting the code comment that a "chat tangent alone is weak evidence".

**A failed lag measurement is shown to the user as a measurement.** `analyzeSignals` keeps `.lagSec` and drops `.measured`, so the Studio prints "reacts 8s late" even when 8 is merely the default. The estimator itself is good. Its direction is correct, and on synthetic data with a true 15 s lag it returned 15 to 17 s in 60 of 60 runs, and declined to claim a measurement in 60 of 60 runs when audio and chat were unrelated. Real streams will be harder.

**The reaction regex needs work before it deserves double weight.** It has no word boundaries, so "clip" matches "eclipse", "lul" matches "lullaby" and "pog" matches "pogo"; it is English-centric and omits common reactions like "gg". `parseLiveChat` prefers `emoji.shortcuts[0]` over the emoji itself, so the literal laughing-face, skull and fire branches may never fire. Check this on the first real replay file, because I could not confirm the payload.

**The judge can run on chat alone.** With an LLM key and no speech-to-text key, candidates reach the model with no transcript. Lindroos et al. found chat-only input ineffective for understanding gaming clips ([Lindroos et al. 2026](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/109439)). Require a speech-to-text key whenever an LLM key is used, or warn clearly. Separately, `transcribe` passes no language hint, so language is detected window by window; Hinglish in particular needs a test ([Trelis model card, self-reported](https://huggingface.co/Trelis/whisper-hinglish-preview)).

Three things should stay out. A kill-feed reader has poor transfer and no evidence on YouTube footage. Sentiment has no gaming F1, and in the one course project that tried LLM excitement ratings on chat windows, the authors admitted they had not evaluated them thoroughly ([HiLAIT](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/2024/papers/hilait-automatic-video-highlighting-system-leveraging-audio-text.pdf)). And a "virality score" should not be built: **no vendor publishes a formula, weights or validation**, and no retrieved source indicates any score uses real post-publication performance.

## CutCut wins on inspectable picks and long-stream cost, and loses on data access

**Selection you can inspect is the strongest opening.** Clip choice is the most repeated complaint found, from at least seven reviewers across listings ([Eklipse Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/eklipse.gg?page=5)), yet no reviewed product documents explaining its choices and StreamLadder states you cannot customise what its AI looks for ([StreamLadder](https://www.streamladder.com/clipgpt/ai-clipping)). CutCut already shows a reason and signal bars per clip. It should go further and show the chat lines and transcript that triggered the clip, then let the user re-weight. Paired with a published evaluation, this attacks a gap every rival leaves open: OpusClip's blog cites "0.93 mAP" with no methodology ([Opus blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-tools-for-precise-video-highlight-search-accuracy)).

**YouTube chat replay with measured lag is real but narrower than it looks.** It is a differentiator against commercial tools only, it relies on unofficial extraction (the official API returns `liveChatEnded`, [YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/live/docs/liveChatMessages/list)), and open-source projects already ingest chat logs. Twitch's Auto Clips shows the platform owner thinks chat matters.

**Cost favours long streams, within limits.** OpusClip's metering works out at $15.54 to $24 for a four-hour stream, against $0.16 to $0.44 for transcription ([calculated from notes](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/eklipse-vs-opusclip-head-to-head-for-gaming-streamers-2026/)). That comparison is not like-for-like because credits also buy rendering and storage, and LLM costs were not established. It is weaker against Eklipse's flat rate. No evidence shows mainstream creators want BYOK; setup friction is real, so CutCut should estimate cost before each run.

**User-owned output and honest low-signal warnings are cheap wins.** Local exports answer the "deleted all my projects" complaints ([OpusClip Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/opus.pro?page=4)). A warning that a stream has little chat or commentary, plus a fast keep/reject screen with cut-point nudging, addresses "needed to be adjusted and sorted" ([Eklipse Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/eklipse.gg?page=5)). No tool was found that warns about in-game music risk, so a per-clip notice is open, provided it never claims to predict Content ID.

**Where CutCut cannot win.** YouTube's AI highlights needs no download, no key and no Terms risk, and if it reaches the creators CutCut targets it will win on convenience; its quality is unknown, so compete on control and gaming-specific selection rather than on "automatic Shorts exist". Exact game events are unavailable on finished video, so on supported titles Medal or Overwolf will always be more precise about what happened. Eklipse's per-game models will likely beat CutCut on kills in shooters (Inference). Medal's clip corpus, 2 billion videos a year by TechCrunch's count, is a training asset CutCut cannot match ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/16/general-intuition-lands-134m-seed-to-teach-agents-spatial-reasoning-using-video-game-clips)). And silent, low-chat streams are structurally hard: with no voice and no chat, CutCut has nothing to rank until vision is added.

## Build the test set before tuning anything

**The first real run comes before any scoring change.** The pipeline has never touched a real YouTube video, so assumptions about the chat payload, heatmap availability and lag remain unchecked.

| Step | Work | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run end to end on 3 real VODs (one large chat, one small chat, one no-facecam). Log every per-second signal and candidate | Chat emoji parsing, heatmap presence and measured-lag rate are known |
| 2 | Fix the flagged code: chat reliability weighting, smooth agreement, keep `measured` and show it, lag default near 15 s, regex word boundaries, require a transcript for judging | Unit tests cover thin-chat and unmeasured-lag cases |
| 3 | Build the labelled set (below) and record baselines | Random, audio-only, chat-only and current fusion are scored |
| 4 | Add chat repetition, then speech-gated voice, one at a time | Each is kept only if it improves held-out recall or precision |
| 5 | Full transcript with spoken triggers; finalist comparison pass | Precision at the clip count improves; cost per run is logged |
| 6 | Fit global weights with logistic regression; add the scene filter | Weights beat the placeholders on held-out videos |
| 7 | Review screen and transparent reasons; cost estimate before runs | Median review time per run is measured |
| 8 | Vision check on finalists as an optional key; owner retention only after counsel reviews API terms | Measured gain justifies the cost |

**The labelled set can be small because the first decisions are coarse.** About 12 to 15 real VODs, sampled to include large and small chat, shooters and reaction-heavy games such as horror, facecam and no-facecam, and Hinglish if that audience matters. Two people independently mark good moments in a fixed two-hour stretch of each VOD, tagging them funny, action or interaction, a split that fits what Ringer found on PUBG. Measure agreement between labellers first, since one editor's labels were a stated limit in Song 2016. Keep a third of VODs untouched for final comparison.

Three metrics answer different questions. **Recall on the shortlist** (share of labelled moments inside a candidate window) tunes the weights. **Precision at K** (share of the final clips a blind rater would post) judges the LLM stage. **Worst-video performance** catches streams where everything fails, which is what Ringer's per-video spread warns about. Add boundary quality (does the clip include setup and payoff) and, once used by real people, keep rate, cut-point adjustments and review time. LIGHTOR's public Dota and LoL labels provide a cheap sanity check on chat features ([repo](https://github.com/sfu-db/Lightor)). Do not evaluate on Most Replayed while also using it as an input, since that is circular. With this few videos only large differences will show, so report results per video and treat a brand partner's unaudited estimate of about 60% usable clips on gaming as a rough floor ([Two Average Gamers](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/eklipse-vs-opusclip-head-to-head-for-gaming-streamers-2026/)).

## Policy text creates real exposure around yt-dlp, music and repeated Shorts

The following reports policy wording and is not legal advice.

**YouTube access.** YouTube's Terms bar users from "access, reproduce, download, distribute" content except as expressly authorised or with prior written permission from YouTube and, where applicable, rights holders, and bar access "using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" ([YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/static?template=terms)). CutCut's ingest, including chat replay and the `YTDLP_COOKIES` option, runs through yt-dlp. The README notes that hosting would need cookies and a residential proxy, which increases exposure (Inference). No court ruling or YouTube statement specific to commercial yt-dlp products was found.

**API policies.** API clients must not "download, import, backup, cache, or store copies of YouTube audiovisual content without YouTube's prior written approval" (III.E.1.a) or "separate, isolate, or modify the audio or video components" (III.I.7) ([Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)). Combining an OAuth API client for retention data with yt-dlp in the same product creates a tension that a policy audit could expose (Inference). Users "must have final control over the data that will be published" (III.C.3), which fits a human review step before anything is posted. Lower-exposure designs are a local-only tool or user-supplied files, but neither removes the Terms text, and a channel owner's rights in the content do not replace YouTube's permission under that wording.

**Copyright.** Nintendo's guidelines bar "mere copies of Nintendo Game Content without creative input or commentary" ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/networkservice_guideline/en/index.html)), which matters for automated clips, although whether auto-captions count as creative input is unaddressed. Current official policies for Rockstar, Riot, Krafton/BGMI, Microsoft/Mojang and Activision were not read, and neither was YouTube's Content ID documentation.

**Shorts monetisation.** Inauthentic content covers "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Reused content reviews "commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos" and "applies even if you have permission from the original creator" ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). The text read does not settle whether clips of a creator's own stream are at risk. Many near-identical Shorts in one caption template is the likelier problem, so template variety and human review are sensible mitigations, though no text says they suffice. Reports of an October 2026 Shorts change and an August 2026 Partner Program overhaul could not be confirmed against official sources.

## What this research could not establish

**Reddit evidence could not be gathered.** The search tool refused the domain and unfiltered searches returned no usable threads, so r/NewTubers, r/Twitch and similar communities are unrepresented; two stray Reddit comments that surfaced in the notes through mirror sites are not used here. A person should read them before treating the complaint themes as complete. App-store reviews and creator forums were also largely unreached. Trustpilot skews toward billing events and few reviewers are specifically gaming or YouTube users.

**The quantitative evidence is from the wrong setting.** Most numbers are LoL or Dota esports broadcasts with casters and large audiences. Three-way fusion results come from one dataset. The only personality-stream results involve two PUBG streamers. Nothing was found on variety, IRL or Just Chatting formats, and no study tests any of this on YouTube streams, whose chat culture and latency differ from Twitch (Inference). There is no published F1 or mAP for GPT, Gemini or Claude on gaming highlights, and no neutral same-footage comparison of commercial tools on commentary-free gameplay.

**Prices are partly unconfirmed.** Medal's pricing page returned 404. Prices for Allstar, Outplayed, StreamLadder, Vizard's paid tiers, Munch, CapCut and Sizzle could not be confirmed, Eklipse's figures conflict across listings, and OpenAI transcription pricing returned 403. LLM cost per stream was not established. BYOK absence for commercial products was not exhaustively checked.

**Attribution corrections and exclusions.** The EMNLP 2017 dataset paper is **Fu, Lee, Bansal and Berg 2017, not "Han et al."** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1102/)). No dataset called GameStream was found. Several widely repeated statistics on hooks and short-form retention could not be traced to a source and are excluded, as is one reported link between virality score and views. Many tool details come from search-result summaries rather than opened pages; eklipse.gg blocked direct fetches, so Eklipse's first-party text is second-hand. Availability of YouTube's AI highlights is unverified, and whether Most Replayed appears on a given stream depends on view thresholds no official source states.

## Conclusion

The research reframes CutCut's problem. Factor choice matters less than it first appears, because the weights only fill a shortlist and an LLM makes the final call; what will decide quality is whether the shortlist contains the moment and whether the judge has enough evidence to recognise it. That makes the chat-density gate, the speech-gated voice signal and the transcript more valuable than any new detector.

The competitive position follows from the same logic. Tools with game data know what happened and tools with scale know what people clip, while CutCut can know only how people reacted. That is a narrower claim than most vendors make, but it is one CutCut can show on screen and measure in public, and no competitor found has done either.
